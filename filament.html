Sustainability

Closed-loop 3D printer filament

Student Environmental Resource Center, UC Berkeley · Jan 2026 to present · PLA recycling, granulation, extrusion

The problem

UC Berkeley has more than a hundred 3D printers across campus, and the project's own working estimate is that around half of all prints fail. Those failures are PLA. It is technically recyclable and almost never actually recycled, so it goes to landfill while the campus keeps buying new filament at full price.

The loop we are building

Our team is working to develop a closed loop system within UC Berkeley where filament scraps can be collected, recycled, reused and redistributed. Failed prints are granulated into pellets, dried, then melted and extruded together with virgin PLA into new filament thread, which is cooled and spooled for campus makerspaces to use.

What I work on

I work on the recycling side of that loop, granulating scraps and then melting and extruding them to create our own filament.

Why extrusion is the hard part

Filament has to hold a consistent diameter or a printer jams on it, and that diameter depends on temperature, extrusion speed, how fast the thread cools and how fast the spool pulls it. The team is also still working out the right ratio of recycled to virgin PLA, and how far the material degrades each time it goes around the loop.

Who runs it

The project sits under the Student Environmental Resource Center and the Zero Waste Research Center at UC Berkeley, and has been supported by The Green Initiative Fund since 2016.
