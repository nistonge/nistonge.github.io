Research

Artificial Coral Project

Gu Research Group, UC Berkeley · Nov 2025 to present · MATLAB, Smoothed Particle Hydrodynamics (SPH)

The work

Our research group aims to produce an optimal artificial coral structure that serves as both a means of wave dissipation and encourages the settlement of polyps and natural coral growth. Using MATLAB, we constructed a computational coral growth model to simulate various coral geometries. These structures are then tested and ranked using simulations and experimental tests. My role on this team has primarily been developing and finalizing our growth model. I simplified the generator to simply require 5 input parameters: slenderness, complexity, spread angle, dominance and blobbiness. With just these 5 inputs, a broad range of structures can be generated.

What it showed

Our team has also begun preliminary force and stress tests to study the effects of geometry on coral stability. Ocean waves and currents move at high velocities and our structures must be able to handle the resulting forces. A material strength of 10 MPa is used as a placeholder until we determine a more accurate strength for calcium carbonate. For future tests, we plan to rank coral structures with a score that rewards large frontal area, while keeping force and mass low. Our early results show slender coral experiencing larger force and stress, but more realistic SPH simulations will be run to validate these predictions. Additionally, the highest stress occurs in the trunk, just above the mount for each coral. Although we are still early in the testing process, the high factors of safety indicate that decreasing volume fraction for increased permeability and light access is possible.

Also on this project

The geometries feed a smoothed particle hydrodynamics wave flume, with a paddle wavemaker at one end, the coral on the floor, energy probes upstream and downstream, and a damping buffer at the far end to stop waves reflecting back into the measurement. Every archived run in the group repository used a damping strength of 20, but nobody had checked whether that was right or simply the first value that worked. I ran the first sweep of it, at 2.5, 5, 10, 20 and 40 in an empty tank, and measured how much wave energy each setting absorbed. A strength of 20 sits on the flat part of the absorption curve, so I recommended keeping it, with a reason behind it rather than an inheritance.

Print quality control

The group also prints lattice test structures in boron carbide on an Oasis binder jet printer. Binder jetting leaves defects that are easy to miss by eye at this scale, so I segment photographs of the build plate to binary and use the result to identify print errors before parts go on to testing.
